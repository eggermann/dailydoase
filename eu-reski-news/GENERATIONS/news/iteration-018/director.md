## Derive the scene

1. Extract facts stated by the article. Observe the news image separately: visible objects, space, light, gesture, and composition. Explicitly note if it is a logo, stock photo, illustration, or unrelated image. Do not infer identities or invisible events from a photograph.
2. Assess each semantic-stream entry against the actual article and image. Use its surrounding sentences as well as its title. The stream is a source of creative associations, not a fact-matching test: an entry need not be named in the article to contribute. Explore whether it can introduce an invented action, material transformation or spatial tension that makes the article's conflict clearer. Explain the causal bridge from the fragment to that action. Separate this invented metaphor from all factual claims; never imply the article reported it. Do not invent a connection because two words can be loosely associated. Mark an entry relevant only when it can shape a specific, coherent story beat. Do not reject a fragment solely because its country or subject is not mentioned in the article. Equally, do not mark it relevant solely because it shares the article's country: its contribution must change a visible action or relationship. Read thematic details such as terrain, movement, institutions, changing boundaries or collective decision-making in the fragment and connect one of them to the article's situation, when that connection is coherent. At least one relevant stream entry MUST materially change the story or its visual action. Give each relevant entry a distinctive visual motif and assign it to scene(s); each stillPrompt MUST repeat that exact motif phrase and turn it into a visible action or spatial relationship. A motif must be more specific than a generic team, flag, color, national stereotype, stars, map outline, or abstract "unity". If no entry can be connected honestly, stop and say so in the plan's premise; do not fabricate a connection. Explain unrelated entries as not relevant, briefly and plainly. Stream links are fictional metaphors, never article facts.
3. Number scenes consecutively from 1 through sceneCount. Invent a specific premise and a coherent sequence of the requested number of scenes. Let the article's situation and semantic associations determine setting, action, emotional tension, and composition. Never reuse a predetermined courtroom, memorial hall, or stock plot across articles. Do not force suffering or war into unrelated news.
4. Select exactly one Guernica catalog figure per scene, by its id. Explain why its gesture or shape fits that scene. Catalog interpretations are possible readings, not established facts. Keep recurring characters visually consistent. Include the recurring fictional Guernica-style crocodile according to the character rules below.
5. Use the supplied `sceneDurations` array as authoritative: copy the matching integer into each scene's `durationSeconds` exactly. These values come from the persistent balanced Taktmuster stream, not from your own timing choice. Write a separate `videoPrompt` for later image-to-video generation. Begin from the exact generated first frame and describe one continuous, physically plausible action that develops across that scene's exact duration, plus restrained camera movement. Preserve the same figures, composition, and painted surfaces; animate existing forms instead of adding new actors, props, writing, or scene changes. Avoid cuts, transitions, speech, and sound. The video prompt must guide motion and must not restate the still-image prompt. Write it in English. Make each scene's movement distinct and causally connected to its article beat and relevant semantic motif.
## Conditional recurring character: the Guernica-style crocodile

The crocodile is an original recurring character, not a figure in Picasso's original painting and not automatically required in a story. Decide whether it works as a meaningful metaphor for THIS article and story. Include it only when its action contributes to that story or a relevant semantic link. Omit it when it would be a mascot, decoration, hidden Easter egg, arbitrary witness, or distraction. Mark `crocodileAppearance.present` honestly; use empty role and placement when absent. Do not force a crocodile merely to satisfy a quota.

When it fits, keep its recognizable design: long low angular snout, jagged geometric back, short sturdy legs, fractured charcoal-black and stained-ivory planes, one small worn-gold scale. Let its action and placement follow from the story. Make it visibly participate in the composition, never a tiny edge decoration. Paint and light it as part of the same scene. It needs no separate reference image.

## Compose the final stillPrompt

Write a complete, concrete, self-contained image prompt in natural English, roughly 220–330 words per scene. Translate every relevant article detail and semantic motif into English; do not leave German, Romanian, Russian, or other source-language words in the prompt. Keep non-English wording in the source and explanation fields only. The result MUST read immediately as a visibly hand-painted, aggressively abstracted Guernica-inspired cubist mural, never as a photograph or photorealistic illustration. Apply abstraction to human faces, bodies, clothing, architecture and objects, not only to the background. Explicitly mention "reference image 1" and "reference image 2" with their different roles in every stillPrompt. Describe the source scene as fully repainted, not realistic photography. Keep even a daytime setting dark and sooty; translate a bright sky into stained ivory or ash gray instead of vivid blue. Do not reproduce the news subject's recognizable face or any real person. Replace people with fictional, mask-like fractured cubist figures, asymmetrical faces, elongated or angular limbs, compressed proportions and visibly brush-worked planes. Do not copy readable lettering, club crests, badges, logos, numbers, brand marks or exact uniforms from the photograph; invent abstract nonverbal shapes instead. The photo supplies only broad composition or atmosphere. It will go directly to the selected image API, without a human rewriting it. Describe one first-frame still, a frozen moment; no panels, video, audio, captions, or text rendering. Each scene is planned for at least five seconds of later screen time, but only still images are generated.

The image API always receives exactly TWO references for each scene:
- Reference image 1 is the original news photograph. Use relevant observed spatial, lighting, material, or gesture cues. Do not invent factual evidence or reproduce an identifiable person as the fictional protagonist.
- Reference image 2 is that scene's selected Guernica fragment. It is a guide to identity, expressive gesture, and fractured anatomy ONLY. Redraw the figure fully, with a newly invented body, clothing, scale, pose details, light, and surface appropriate to this scene. Do not refer to catalog image numbers in the stillPrompt; use reference image 2 regardless of selected id.

Give the figure a definite location, scale, action, and relationship to surrounding objects. Describe a support surface/contact shadow, an occlusion or shared reflection, and one common directional light appropriate to the setting. Preserve recognizability through gesture and anatomy while embedding the figure physically into the new space. No copied crop, mask edge, transparency outline, original background, framing, or pasted texture. The news setting must also be repainted, not left as a photograph behind a drawing.

## Keep the same painted language across scenes

Treat the sequence as different moments painted by one artist on the same aged mural. Establish one degree of facial and anatomical abstraction, one palette, one pigment grain and one brushstroke scale; carry these through EVERY scene even when a different catalog figure is selected. Change action and composition according to the story, while preserving this painted language.

Build every face from displaced angular planes: an off-axis eye, a wedge-shaped nose, an asymmetrical mask-like mouth and broken cheek planes. Construct hands, limbs and garments from equally fractured shapes. Render hair as scratched charcoal masses, skin as stained ivory and ash-gray pigment with visible dry brush. Flatten volume into intersecting painted planes; facial detail must stay as coarse and abstract as the surrounding architecture. A selected woman, an injured protagonist and a helper must all receive the same treatment. Avoid a naturalistic portrait embedded in an abstract setting, smooth flesh shading, detailed individual hair strands and finely modeled facial features. Preserve emotion through angular gesture and composition.

Repeat the following shared style paragraph verbatim in EVERY stillPrompt, alongside the scene-specific action and reference roles:

"One original, dark Guernica-like cubist mural, painted as a compressed horizontal frieze in shallow, fractured space. Build the whole image from large interlocking black, ash-gray and stained-ivory planes, sharp charcoal contours, rough oil-paint scumbling and scratched plaster. Distort every human face into a blunt mask with mismatched, displaced features; reduce bodies, hands, clothing and objects to flat angular signs and broken silhouettes. Show emotion through pose and symbolic gesture, never realistic facial expression or anatomy. Keep the image near-monochrome; no saturated color, neon screens, photographic detail, naturalistic skin, smooth volume, realistic perspective, cinematic lighting or 3D depth. Every element must look painted by the same hand and belong to the same fractured mural."

Before returning the plan, compare all stillPrompts. Rewrite any prompt whose protagonist receives more realistic facial detail, smoother painting or a different visual treatment than the other scenes. Keep the chosen story beats and meaningful semantic relationships intact.

For each `videoPrompt`, state what moves first, how the selected character or significant object responds, and how the motion settles by the end. Keep visible lettering already present in a start image frozen and unchanged; do not ask the video model to add, replace, or animate text.
